# Image references — MYP 1 Mathematics, Improved First Edition

## What is in this folder

| File | What it records |
|---|---|
| `figure_inventory.csv` | Every figure in the book: its id, the manuscript file that places it, its caption, its origin, and the exported files in `working/` |

## Position on images

This edition uses **no photographs, no clip art and no third-party artwork**. Every figure is an
original vector drawing generated by the build itself (`manuscript/engine/bookkit/figures.py`, wired
through `figs_registry.py`), so there is nothing to license, clear or attribute. That is a deliberate
decision, and it is also why the book can be redistributed and edited freely.

- **48 distinct drawings**, 46 of them placed in the manuscript (50 placements, because four figures
  are reused where the same picture serves two explanations).
- Two drawings are registered but not placed: `c01_round10` and `c06_machine`. They remain available
  for a future edition.
- Each drawing is exported at build time to `working/fig_<id>.pdf` (vector) and
  `working/fig_<id>.png` (200 dpi raster, used by the DOCX exporter), so a designer can reuse any
  figure outside the book.

## If a future edition adds photographs

The drawings were chosen where a photograph would add nothing (a hexagon split into triangles) and
where a photograph would be *less* clear (a place-value table). Where a photograph would genuinely
help — the museum label of Chapter 1, the water tank of Chapter 9, the canteen survey of Chapter 10 —
the manuscript places a drawing so that the page is complete without a picture. To substitute a
photograph:

1. Add the image to `working/` as a 300 dpi JPG or PNG.
2. In `figs_registry.py`, replace the lambda for that figure id with a function returning an
   `Image` flowable at the same width, and keep the caption text (captions are used by the DOCX
   export and by this inventory).
3. Record the licence and the source in this folder in a new CSV column, and add the credit line to
   `frontmatter.acknowledgements()`.

## Caption policy

Every figure is captioned with what it shows and what it is for, in one sentence, and captions are
printed under the drawing in 7.5 pt. The figure is never the only carrier of a fact: the surrounding
prose states the result as well, so the book remains usable in a photocopy or on a screen reader.
