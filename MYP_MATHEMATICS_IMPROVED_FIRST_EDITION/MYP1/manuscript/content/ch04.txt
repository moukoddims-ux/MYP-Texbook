@SEC | 4.1 | What a fraction means | Criterion A · i, ii
@LEAD | A fraction can be read as a part of a whole, as a division, as an operator on a quantity, or as a position on a number line. Reading it in more than one way prevents most errors.
@DEF | Fraction, numerator, denominator
In the fraction $\\frac{a}{b}$ ($b \\neq 0$), $a$ is the **numerator** and $b$ is the **denominator**. The denominator says how many equal parts the whole is divided into; the numerator says how many of those parts are taken.
A **proper fraction** has numerator smaller than denominator, so its value is less than 1. An **improper fraction** has numerator greater than or equal to the denominator, so its value is at least 1. A **mixed number** is a whole number and a proper fraction written together, such as $2\\frac{3}{5}$.
@KEY | Four readings of $\\frac{3}{4}$
@LISTU
- **Part of a whole:** three of four equal parts.
- **Division:** $3 \\div 4$.
- **Operator:** three quarters of a quantity — $\\frac{3}{4}$ of 20 is 15.
- **Number:** a point on the number line, three quarters of the way from 0 to 1.
@P | The fourth reading matters most later: $\\frac{3}{4}$ is a single number that can be added, multiplied and placed on a line, not a sum of two numbers.
@METHOD | Converting between mixed numbers and improper fractions
@LISTO
1. Mixed to improper: multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the denominator. $2\\frac{3}{5} = \\frac{2 \\times 5 + 3}{5} = \\frac{13}{5}$.
2. Improper to mixed: divide the numerator by the denominator; the quotient is the whole number, the remainder goes over the denominator. $\\frac{17}{4} = 4\\frac{1}{4}$.
3. Check by estimating: $\\frac{13}{5}$ is between 2 and 3, which matches $2\\frac{3}{5}$.
@ERR | Adding the whole numbers to the denominator
A common error is to write $2\\frac{3}{5} = \\frac{5}{5}$, adding 2 and 3. The whole number 2 means two whole units, each of which is $\\frac{5}{5}$, so the total is $\\frac{10}{5} + \\frac{3}{5} = \\frac{13}{5}$.

@EX | 4.1.1 | Reading a fraction in context
GIVEN: A 600 ml bottle is $\\frac{3}{5}$ full.
ASKED: How much liquid is in the bottle? Explain using two readings of the fraction.
METHOD: Use the fraction as an operator: divide by the denominator, multiply by the numerator.
STEPS:
1. Divide 600 ml into 5 equal parts: $600 \\div 5 = 120$ ml per part.
2. Take 3 parts: $3 \\times 120 = 360$ ml.
3. Reading it as a multiplication: $\\frac{3}{5} \\times 600 = 360$.
ANSWER: 360 ml.
CHECK: $\\frac{3}{5}$ is more than half, and 360 ml is more than 300 ml.

@EX | 4.1.2 | Mixed numbers and improper fractions
GIVEN: The mixed number $3\\frac{2}{7}$ and the improper fraction $\\frac{23}{6}$.
ASKED: Write each in the other form.
METHOD: Apply the two conversion rules and check by estimation.
STEPS:
1. $3\\frac{2}{7} = \\frac{3 \\times 7 + 2}{7} = \\frac{23}{7}$.
2. $23 \\div 6 = 3$ remainder 5, so $\\frac{23}{6} = 3\\frac{5}{6}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{23}{7}$ and $3\\frac{5}{6}$.
CHECK: $\\frac{23}{7}$ is between 3 and 4; $3\\frac{5}{6}$ is between 3 and 4.

@EX | 4.1.3 | A fraction of a quantity with a remainder
GIVEN: A class of 32 students; $\\frac{5}{8}$ walk to school.
ASKED: How many walk, and how many do not?
METHOD: Find one eighth first, then scale.
STEPS:
1. One eighth of 32 is $32 \\div 8 = 4$.
2. Five eighths is $5 \\times 4 = 20$ students.
3. The remainder is $32 - 20 = 12$, which is $\\frac{3}{8}$ of the class.
ANSWER: 20 walk; 12 do not.
CHECK: $\\frac{3}{8} \\times 32 = 12$.
WATCH: The denominator tells you the number of parts, not the size of the class. Always divide the quantity by the denominator first.

@SEC | 4.2 | Equivalent fractions and simplifying | Criterion A · ii, iii
@LEAD | Equivalent fractions are different names for the same number. Simplifying chooses the simplest name; expanding chooses a convenient one. Both are done by multiplying or dividing top and bottom by the same number.
@DEF | Equivalent fractions and simplest form
Fractions are **equivalent** when they represent the same value, such as $\\frac{2}{3}$ and $\\frac{4}{6}$. Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero whole number produces an equivalent fraction.
A fraction is in **simplest form** when the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1.
@KEY | Why the value does not change
$\\frac{2}{3} = \\frac{2 \\times 2}{3 \\times 2} = \\frac{4}{6}$. Multiplying both parts by 2 cuts every third into two sixths: the number of pieces doubles and each piece halves, so the total is unchanged. The same reasoning explains division: $\\frac{6}{9} = \\frac{6 \\div 3}{9 \\div 3} = \\frac{2}{3}$ groups the pieces in threes.
@METHOD | Simplifying in one step
@LISTO
1. Find the highest common factor of the numerator and denominator.
2. Divide both by it.
3. Check that the result is in simplest form by testing for a common factor again.
@P | Using the HCF does the job in one step. Dividing repeatedly by small factors also works and can be safer when the HCF is hard to see.
@TABLE | Simplifying with the HCF
Fraction ; HCF of numerator and denominator ; Simplest form ; Check
$\\frac{18}{24}$ ; 6 ; $\\frac{3}{4}$ ; $3 \\times 6 = 18$, $4 \\times 6 = 24$
$\\frac{45}{60}$ ; 15 ; $\\frac{3}{4}$ ; both divide by 15
$\\frac{28}{42}$ ; 14 ; $\\frac{2}{3}$ ; 28 = 14 × 2, 42 = 14 × 3
$\\frac{51}{68}$ ; 17 ; $\\frac{3}{4}$ ; 51 = 17 × 3, 68 = 17 × 4
@ERR | Dividing only one part
$\\frac{8}{12}$ is not $\\frac{2}{12}$: dividing the numerator alone changes the value. Both parts must be divided by the same number. A useful check is to compare the decimal values before and after; they must match.

@EX | 4.2.1 | Simplifying
GIVEN: The fraction $\\frac{56}{72}$.
ASKED: Write it in simplest form.
METHOD: Find the HCF of 56 and 72 and divide both parts.
STEPS:
1. $56 = 2^{3} \\times 7$ and $72 = 2^{3} \\times 3^{2}$, so the HCF is $2^{3} = 8$.
2. $\\frac{56 \\div 8}{72 \\div 8} = \\frac{7}{9}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{7}{9}$.
CHECK: 7 and 9 share no factor other than 1; $56 \\div 72 = 0.777\\dots = 7 \\div 9$.

@EX | 4.2.2 | Finding a missing part
GIVEN: $\\frac{3}{7} = \\frac{\\square}{42}$.
ASKED: Find the missing numerator.
METHOD: Ask what the denominator was multiplied by, then do the same to the numerator.
STEPS:
1. $7 \\times 6 = 42$, so the multiplier is 6.
2. $3 \\times 6 = 18$.
ANSWER: 18, so $\\frac{3}{7} = \\frac{18}{42}$.
CHECK: $18 \\div 42 = 0.42857$ and $3 \\div 7 = 0.42857$.

@EX | 4.2.3 | Simplifying in context
GIVEN: A survey found that 48 out of 120 students walk to school.
ASKED: Write the proportion as a fraction in simplest form.
METHOD: Write the fraction, then simplify with the HCF.
STEPS:
1. Fraction: $\\frac{48}{120}$.
2. HCF of 48 and 120 is 24.
3. $\\frac{48 \\div 24}{120 \\div 24} = \\frac{2}{5}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{2}{5}$ of the students.
CHECK: $\\frac{2}{5} \\times 120 = 48$.

@SEC | 4.3 | Comparing and ordering fractions | Criterion C · ii, iii
@LEAD | Comparing fractions is comparing numbers, and there is more than one reliable method. Choosing the method to suit the numbers is part of the skill.
@METHOD | Four methods for comparing fractions
@LISTO
1. **Common denominator:** rewrite both fractions with the LCM of the denominators, then compare numerators.
2. **Common numerator:** if the numerators are equal, the fraction with the smaller denominator is larger.
3. **Benchmark:** compare each fraction with a familiar value such as $\\frac{1}{2}$ or 1.
4. **Decimal:** divide to compare as decimals when the numbers are awkward.
@KEY | Choosing a method
Common denominators give an exact answer and show the size of the difference. Decimals are quick but can require rounding, which risks a wrong conclusion when two fractions are very close — $\\frac{7}{16}$ and $\\frac{11}{25}$ differ by only 0.0025.
@TABLE | Ordering four fractions
Fraction ; Decimal ; Comparison with $\\frac{1}{2}$
$\\frac{5}{8}$ ; 0.625 ; above
$\\frac{2}{3}$ ; 0.667 ; above
$\\frac{7}{10}$ ; 0.7 ; above
$\\frac{3}{4}$ ; 0.75 ; above
@P | All four are above $\\frac{1}{2}$, so the benchmark alone cannot order them. With a common denominator of 120: $\\frac{75}{120}, \\frac{80}{120}, \\frac{84}{120}, \\frac{90}{120}$, giving $\\frac{5}{8} < \\frac{2}{3} < \\frac{7}{10} < \\frac{3}{4}$.
@ERR | Comparing numerators only
$\\frac{3}{8}$ and $\\frac{2}{5}$: the numerators 3 and 2 do not decide which is larger, because the denominators differ. With a common denominator of 40, $\\frac{15}{40}$ and $\\frac{16}{40}$, so $\\frac{2}{5}$ is larger — the opposite of what the numerators suggest.

@EX | 4.3.1 | Comparing two fractions
GIVEN: $\\frac{5}{8}$ and $\\frac{7}{12}$.
ASKED: State which is larger and by how much.
METHOD: Use the LCM of the denominators.
STEPS:
1. LCM of 8 and 12 is 24.
2. $\\frac{5}{8} = \\frac{15}{24}$ and $\\frac{7}{12} = \\frac{14}{24}$.
3. The difference is $\\frac{1}{24}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{5}{8}$ is larger by $\\frac{1}{24}$.
CHECK: As decimals, 0.625 > 0.583.

@EX | 4.3.2 | Ordering three fractions
GIVEN: $\\frac{3}{5}$, $\\frac{5}{9}$, $\\frac{7}{12}$.
ASKED: Write them in ascending order.
METHOD: Convert to decimals because the denominators are unrelated.
STEPS:
1. $\\frac{3}{5} = 0.6$.
2. $\\frac{5}{9} = 0.555\\dots$
3. $\\frac{7}{12} = 0.5833\\dots$
4. Ordering: 0.555 < 0.583 < 0.6.
ANSWER: $\\frac{5}{9} < \\frac{7}{12} < \\frac{3}{5}$.
CHECK: With a common denominator of 180: $\\frac{100}{180} < \\frac{105}{180} < \\frac{108}{180}$.

@EX | 4.3.3 | Comparing with a mixed number
GIVEN: $2\\frac{2}{3}$ and $\\frac{17}{6}$.
ASKED: Decide which is larger.
METHOD: Write both as improper fractions over a common denominator.
STEPS:
1. $2\\frac{2}{3} = \\frac{8}{3} = \\frac{16}{6}$.
2. Compare $\\frac{16}{6}$ with $\\frac{17}{6}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{17}{6}$ is larger by $\\frac{1}{6}$.
CHECK: $2\\frac{2}{3} \\approx 2.667$ and $\\frac{17}{6} \\approx 2.833$.
WATCH: Comparing only the fractional parts (both thirds or sixths) without the whole numbers gives the wrong order.

@SEC | 4.4 | Fractions and decimals | Criterion A · ii; Criterion C · ii
@LEAD | Every fraction can be written as a decimal by division, and every terminating decimal can be written as a fraction over a power of ten. Some fractions produce decimals that never end but repeat.
@FIG | c04_division
@DEF | Terminating and recurring decimals
A **terminating decimal** has a finite number of digits after the point, such as $0.375$. A **recurring decimal** repeats a block of digits for ever, such as $0.333\\dots$ where the 3 never stops. This book writes the repeating block twice and ends with an ellipsis: $0.363636\\dots$ for the block 36.
A fraction in simplest form gives a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.
@FIG | c04_grid
@METHOD | Converting in both directions
**Fraction to decimal:** divide the numerator by the denominator, continuing past the point until the remainder is 0 or a cycle appears.
**Terminating decimal to fraction:** write over a power of ten and simplify. $0.45 = \\frac{45}{100} = \\frac{9}{20}$.
**Recurring decimal to fraction:** use the repeating block. If $x = 0.\\dot{3}$, then $10x = 3.\\dot{3}$, so $9x = 3$ and $x = \\frac{1}{3}$.
@KEY | Why 2s and 5s matter
$\\frac{3}{8} = \\frac{3}{2^{3}}$. Multiplying by $\\frac{5^{3}}{5^{3}}$ gives $\\frac{375}{1000} = 0.375$ — a terminating decimal, because 8 divides a power of ten. A denominator with a factor of 3 or 7 never divides a power of ten, so the division must recur.
@TABLE | Fractions, decimals and the reason
Fraction ; Decimal ; Terminating?
$\\frac{3}{8}$ ; 0.375 ; yes, denominator $2^{3}$
$\\frac{7}{20}$ ; 0.35 ; yes, denominator $2^{2} \\times 5$
$\\frac{5}{6}$ ; $0.8\\dot{3}$ ; no, denominator has a factor 3
$\\frac{2}{7}$ ; $0.\\overline{285714}$ ; no, denominator has a factor 7
@ERR | Rounding too early
$\\frac{2}{3} \\approx 0.67$ is fine for reporting, but using 0.67 in a later calculation gives slight errors that accumulate. Keep the fraction, or keep more digits than you need, until the final step.

@EX | 4.4.1 | Fraction to terminating decimal
GIVEN: $\\frac{7}{20}$.
ASKED: Write it as a decimal and explain why it terminates.
METHOD: Scale to a denominator of 100, or divide.
STEPS:
1. $\\frac{7}{20} = \\frac{7 \\times 5}{20 \\times 5} = \\frac{35}{100}$.
2. $\\frac{35}{100} = 0.35$.
3. The denominator $20 = 2^{2} \\times 5$ has only 2s and 5s, so the decimal terminates.
ANSWER: 0.35.
CHECK: $7 \\div 20 = 0.35$.

@EX | 4.4.2 | Fraction to recurring decimal
GIVEN: $\\frac{5}{6}$.
ASKED: Write it as a decimal, using dot notation for any repeating part.
METHOD: Divide and watch for the repeating remainder.
STEPS:
1. $5 \\div 6 = 0.8$ remainder 2.
2. The remainder 2 recurs, giving 0.8333…
3. The digit 3 repeats, so the decimal is $0.8\\dot{3}$.
ANSWER: $0.8\\dot{3}$.
CHECK: Multiplying by 6 gives 4.999…, which equals 5.

@EX | 4.4.3 | Decimal to fraction
GIVEN: 2.6 and $0.\\overline{36}$.
ASKED: Write each as a fraction in simplest form.
METHOD: Use a power of ten for terminating decimals; use the repeating block for recurring ones.
STEPS:
1. $2.6 = \\frac{26}{10} = \\frac{13}{5} = 2\\frac{3}{5}$.
2. For $x = 0.\\overline{36}$: $100x = 36.\\overline{36}$, so $99x = 36$ and $x = \\frac{36}{99} = \\frac{4}{11}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{13}{5}$ and $\\frac{4}{11}$.
CHECK: $4 \\div 11 = 0.363636\\dots$
WATCH: $0.\\overline{36}$ is not $\\frac{36}{100}$; the block repeats for ever, so 99, not 100, is the correct denominator in the subtraction.

@SEC | 4.5 | Calculating with decimals | Criterion A · i, ii; Criterion C · i
@LEAD | Decimal calculations follow the same rules as whole-number calculations, with one extra job: keeping track of where the decimal point belongs. Lining up place values, or counting decimal places, or converting to fractions, all do that job.
@DEF | Aligning place values
To add or subtract decimals, write the numbers one under the other with the **decimal points in a line**, then fill empty places with zeros. Adding zeros after the last digit does not change a number: $4.5 = 4.50 = 4.500$.
@METHOD | Four decimal calculations
**Add or subtract:** line up the points, pad with zeros, then calculate as for whole numbers. $12.4 + 3.75$ becomes $12.40 + 3.75 = 16.15$.
**Multiply by a whole number:** multiply as if there were no point, then place the point so the answer has the same number of decimal places as the number you started with. $0.6 \times 7 = 4.2$.
**Multiply two decimals:** multiply as whole numbers and count the total decimal places in both factors. $0.4 \times 0.3 = 0.12$, because 4 tenths of 3 tenths is 12 hundredths.
**Divide:** make the divisor a whole number by multiplying both numbers by the same power of ten, then divide. $2.4 \div 0.6 = 24 \div 6 = 4$.
@KEY | Multiplying and dividing by 10, 100, 1000
Multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place to the left, so the decimal point appears to move one place to the right: $3.47 \times 10 = 34.7$. Dividing by 10 moves it one place to the left: $3.47 \div 10 = 0.347$. The digits never change; only their place values do. Trailing zeros may be dropped, but zeros between digits may not.
@TABLE | Which operation, which estimate
Calculation ; Quick estimate ; Why the estimate is safe
$8.9 + 12.35$ ; $9 + 12 = 21$ ; the answer must be slightly under 21
$0.7 \times 0.8$ ; about $1 \times 1 = 1$ but under ; two numbers under 1 give a product under 1
$4.5 \div 0.9$ ; about $4.5 \div 1 = 4.5$ ; dividing by less than 1 makes the answer larger
$0.36 \div 4$ ; about $0.4 \div 4 = 0.1$ ; the answer is near one tenth
@ERR | Two decimal-point errors
**Misaligned points.** Writing $12.4 + 3.75$ as $12.4$ over $3.75$ right-aligned gives $15.65$ — wrong, because the 5 of 3.75 then sits in the tenths column. **Counting places wrongly.** $0.2 \times 0.3$ is not 0.6: two decimal places in total means $0.06$.

@EX | 4.5.1 | Adding and subtracting decimals
GIVEN: $7.4 - 2.86$.

METHOD: Line up the decimal points and pad with a zero.
STEPS:

2. $7.40 - 2.86 = 4.54$.

CHECK: $4.54 + 2.86 = 7.40$.

@EX | 4.5.2 | Multiplying two decimals
GIVEN: $1.6 \times 0.25$.

STEPS:

2. There are $1 + 2 = 3$ decimal places in the factors.

@EX | 4.5.3 | Dividing by a decimal
GIVEN: $3.6 \div 0.04$.

STEPS:

@SEC | 4.6 | Adding and subtracting fractions | Criterion A · i, ii
@LEAD | Fractions can only be added when the parts are the same size. Finding a common denominator makes the parts comparable; it does not change the quantities.
@DEF | Adding fractions
To add or subtract fractions, write them with a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator:
$\\frac{a}{c} + \\frac{b}{c} = \\frac{a + b}{c}$
@KEY | Why not add the denominators?
$\\frac{1}{2} + \\frac{1}{3}$ is not $\\frac{2}{5}$: a half plus a third is more than a half, while $\\frac{2}{5}$ is less than a half. With sixths, $\\frac{3}{6} + \\frac{2}{6} = \\frac{5}{6}$, which is close to 1 and passes the estimation check.
@METHOD | Adding and subtracting mixed numbers
@LISTO
1. Convert to improper fractions, or handle whole numbers and fractions separately.
2. Use the LCM of the denominators.
3. Add or subtract; simplify the result; convert back to a mixed number if needed.
4. If subtracting produces a negative fraction, borrow one whole unit from the whole number.
@TABLE | Worked steps for $3\\frac{1}{4} - 1\\frac{5}{6}$
Stage ; Work
Convert ; $\\frac{13}{4} - \\frac{11}{6}$
Common denominator ; LCM of 4 and 6 is 12: $\\frac{39}{12} - \\frac{22}{12}$
Subtract ; $\\frac{17}{12}$
Simplify ; $1\\frac{5}{12}$
@ERR | Adding numerators and denominators
“$\\frac{1}{2} + \\frac{1}{3} = \\frac{2}{5}$” is the most common fraction error. The check is estimation: the answer must lie between the two fractions. $\\frac{2}{5} = 0.4$ lies below both, so it cannot be right.

@EX | 4.6.1 | Adding fractions with unlike denominators
GIVEN: $\\frac{2}{3} + \\frac{1}{4}$.
ASKED: Find the sum in simplest form.
METHOD: Use the LCM of the denominators.
STEPS:
1. LCM of 3 and 4 is 12.
2. $\\frac{2}{3} = \\frac{8}{12}$ and $\\frac{1}{4} = \\frac{3}{12}$.
3. $\\frac{8}{12} + \\frac{3}{12} = \\frac{11}{12}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{11}{12}$, already in simplest form.
CHECK: $0.667 + 0.25 = 0.917$, close to $\\frac{11}{12} = 0.917$. The answer is just below 1, as expected.

@EX | 4.6.2 | Subtracting mixed numbers with borrowing
GIVEN: $4\\frac{1}{5} - 2\\frac{2}{3}$.
ASKED: Find the difference.
METHOD: Convert to improper fractions to avoid negative parts.
STEPS:
1. $4\\frac{1}{5} = \\frac{21}{5}$ and $2\\frac{2}{3} = \\frac{8}{3}$.
2. LCM of 5 and 3 is 15: $\\frac{63}{15} - \\frac{40}{15} = \\frac{23}{15}$.
3. $\\frac{23}{15} = 1\\frac{8}{15}$.
ANSWER: $1\\frac{8}{15}$.
CHECK: $4.2 - 2.667 = 1.533$, and $\\frac{23}{15} = 1.533$.
WATCH: Subtracting the whole numbers and then the fractions gives $2 - \\frac{7}{15}$, which invites the sign error $-\\frac{2}{15}$ appearing out of nowhere. Improper fractions avoid it.

@EX | 4.6.3 | A context with three fractions
GIVEN: A student spends $\\frac{1}{4}$ of an evening on mathematics, $\\frac{3}{8}$ on a project and $\\frac{1}{6}$ reading.
ASKED: What fraction of the evening is left?
METHOD: Add the three fractions, then subtract from 1.
STEPS:
1. LCM of 4, 8 and 6 is 24.
2. $\\frac{6}{24} + \\frac{9}{24} + \\frac{4}{24} = \\frac{19}{24}$.
3. $1 - \\frac{19}{24} = \\frac{5}{24}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{5}{24}$ of the evening.
CHECK: $0.25 + 0.375 + 0.167 = 0.792$ and $\\frac{19}{24} = 0.792$.

@SEC | 4.7 | Multiplying and dividing fractions | Criterion C · i, iii
@LEAD | Multiplication of fractions answers “a part of a part”, and division answers “how many of these fit inside that”. Both are easier than addition, because no common denominator is needed.
@DEF | Multiplying and dividing fractions
$\\frac{a}{b} \\times \\frac{c}{d} = \\frac{ac}{bd}$
$\\frac{a}{b} \\div \\frac{c}{d} = \\frac{a}{b} \\times \\frac{d}{c}$
To divide by a fraction, multiply by its **reciprocal** — the fraction turned upside down.
@KEY | Two pictures that explain the rules
**Multiplication:** $\\frac{2}{3} \\times \\frac{3}{4}$ means two thirds of three quarters. Take a square, cut it into quarters and shade three, then cut the square into thirds and take two of them. The overlap is 6 of 12 small squares, or $\\frac{1}{2}$.
**Division:** $\\frac{3}{4} \\div \\frac{1}{8}$ asks how many eighths fit in three quarters. Each quarter contains two eighths, so there are 6.
@TABLE | Multiplication and division compared
Expression ; Meaning ; Answer
$\\frac{2}{3} \\times \\frac{3}{4}$ ; two thirds of three quarters ; $\\frac{1}{2}$
$\\frac{3}{4} \\div \\frac{1}{8}$ ; how many eighths in three quarters ; 6
$\\frac{3}{4} \\div \\frac{2}{5}$ ; how many two fifths fit in three quarters ; $\\frac{15}{8} = 1\\frac{7}{8}$
@ERR | Inverting the wrong fraction
When dividing, the **second** fraction is inverted: $\\frac{3}{4} \\div \\frac{2}{5} = \\frac{3}{4} \\times \\frac{5}{2}$. Inverting the first fraction gives $\\frac{4}{3} \\times \\frac{2}{5} = \\frac{8}{15}$, which is less than 1 — an impossible answer to “how many $\\frac{2}{5}$ fit in $\\frac{3}{4}$”.

@EX | 4.7.1 | Multiplying fractions
GIVEN: $\\frac{5}{6} \\times \\frac{3}{10}$.
ASKED: Multiply and simplify.
METHOD: Multiply numerators and denominators, then simplify; or cancel common factors first.
STEPS:
1. Cancel: $\\frac{5}{6} \\times \\frac{3}{10} = \\frac{1 \\times 1}{2 \\times 2}$ after dividing 5 and 10 by 5, and 3 and 6 by 3.
2. Result: $\\frac{1}{4}$.
ANSWER: $\\frac{1}{4}$.
CHECK: $0.833 \\times 0.3 = 0.25$.

@EX | 4.7.2 | Dividing by a fraction
GIVEN: $2\\frac{1}{2} \\div 1\\frac{1}{4}$.
ASKED: Find the quotient and interpret the answer.
METHOD: Convert to improper fractions, then multiply by the reciprocal.
STEPS:
1. $2\\frac{1}{2} = \\frac{5}{2}$ and $1\\frac{1}{4} = \\frac{5}{4}$.
2. $\\frac{5}{2} \\times \\frac{4}{5} = \\frac{20}{10} = 2$.
ANSWER: 2, meaning $1\\frac{1}{4}$ fits exactly twice into $2\\frac{1}{2}$.
CHECK: $1\\frac{1}{4} \\times 2 = 2\\frac{1}{2}$.

@EX | 4.7.3 | A fraction of a fraction in context
GIVEN: A school has 480 students. Of these, $\\frac{3}{8}$ are in the first year, and $\\frac{2}{5}$ of the first year walk to school.
ASKED: How many first-year students walk to school?
METHOD: Multiply the fractions to find the overall part, then apply it to the total.
STEPS:
1. $\\frac{3}{8} \\times 480 = 180$ first-year students.
2. $\\frac{2}{5} \\times 180 = 72$.
3. As a single calculation: $\\frac{3}{8} \\times \\frac{2}{5} = \\frac{3}{20}$, and $\\frac{3}{20} \\times 480 = 72$.
ANSWER: 72 students.
CHECK: $\\frac{3}{20}$ of 480 is $480 \\div 20 \\times 3 = 72$.

@MOVE | G | Reflect and consolidate | Summary, vocabulary, misconceptions and review
@SEC | Review | Consolidating Chapter 4 | Criterion A · i, ii, iii
@H2 | What this chapter established
@LISTU
- A fraction is one number with many names; equivalent fractions come from multiplying or dividing both parts by the same number.
- Simplest form uses the HCF; expanding is useful for comparison and for addition.
- Fractions can be compared by common denominators, common numerators, benchmarks or decimals.
- Fractions convert to terminating decimals when the denominator has only factors 2 and 5, and recur otherwise.
- Addition and subtraction need a common denominator; multiplication and division do not.
- Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal.
@H2 | Essential vocabulary

Term ; Meaning in this course
