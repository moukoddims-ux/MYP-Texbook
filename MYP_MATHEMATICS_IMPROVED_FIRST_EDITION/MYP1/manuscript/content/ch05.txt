@SEC | 5.1 | What a percentage is | Criterion A · i, ii
@LEAD | A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100. That single definition explains every conversion, every comparison and every calculation in the chapter.
@DEF | Percentage
A **percentage** is a fraction whose denominator is 100. The symbol % means “out of 100”, so $45\\% = \\frac{45}{100} = 0.45$.
To convert a fraction or decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100. To convert a percentage to a fraction or decimal, divide by 100.
@FIG | c05_grid
@FIG | c05_bar
@KEY | Benchmarks worth knowing by heart
@LISTU
- $1\\% = 0.01$; $10\\% = 0.1$; $50\\% = \\frac{1}{2}$; $25\\% = \\frac{1}{4}$; $75\\% = \\frac{3}{4}$.
- $20\\% = \\frac{1}{5}$; $12.5\\% = \\frac{1}{8}$; $33\\frac{1}{3}\\% = \\frac{1}{3}$; $66\\frac{2}{3}\\% = \\frac{2}{3}$.
- $100\\%$ is the whole; $200\\%$ is double; $150\\%$ is one and a half times.
@METHOD | Converting between the three forms
@LISTO
1. Percentage to decimal: divide by 100 (move the point two places left). $37\\% = 0.37$.
2. Decimal to percentage: multiply by 100 (move the point two places right). $0.6 = 60\\%$.
3. Fraction to percentage: multiply by 100, or scale to a denominator of 100. $\\frac{3}{8} = 37.5\\%$.
4. Percentage to fraction: write over 100 and simplify. $35\\% = \\frac{35}{100} = \\frac{7}{20}$.
@ERR | Moving the wrong way
Multiplying by 100 when converting a percentage to a decimal turns 45% into 4500%, an error of two factors of ten. A useful check: a percentage below 100% must become a decimal below 1.

@EX | 5.1.1 | Converting in both directions
GIVEN: $\\frac{7}{20}$, 0.08 and $62.5\\%$.
ASKED: Write each as a percentage, and each percentage as a fraction in simplest form.
METHOD: Use the definition of percentage as a fraction over 100.
STEPS:
1. $\\frac{7}{20} = \\frac{35}{100} = 35\\%$.
2. $0.08 \\times 100 = 8\\%$.
3. $62.5\\% = \\frac{62.5}{100} = \\frac{625}{1000} = \\frac{5}{8}$.
ANSWER: $35\\%$, $8\\%$, $62.5\\% = \\frac{5}{8}$.
CHECK: $5 \\div 8 = 0.625$, which is $62.5\\%$.

@EX | 5.1.2 | Reading a percentage in context
GIVEN: A school reports that 92% of its 250 students use the library.
ASKED: State the number of students who use the library and the number who do not, then write each as a fraction.
METHOD: Apply the percentage to the total, using the complement for the remainder.
STEPS:
1. $92\\%$ of $250 = 0.92 \\times 250 = 230$.
2. The remainder is $250 - 230 = 20$, which is $8\\%$.
3. As fractions: $\\frac{230}{250} = \\frac{23}{25}$ use it; $\\frac{20}{250} = \\frac{2}{25}$ do not.
ANSWER: 230 use the library; 20 do not.
CHECK: $\\frac{23}{25} + \\frac{2}{25} = 1$, and $230 + 20 = 250$.

@EX | 5.1.3 | A percentage above 100
GIVEN: A club had 40 members last year and 60 this year.
ASKED: Express this year's membership as a percentage of last year's, and describe the change.
METHOD: Divide the new value by the base and multiply by 100.
STEPS:
1. $\\frac{60}{40} = 1.5$.
2. $1.5 \\times 100 = 150\\%$.
ANSWER: The membership is 150% of last year's, an increase of 50%.
CHECK: A 50% increase on 40 is $40 \\times 1.5 = 60$.

@SEC | 5.2 | Finding a percentage of a quantity | Criterion A · i; Criterion C · i, iii
@LEAD | Finding a percentage of a quantity is a multiplication. Three methods give the same answer; the multiplier method is fastest and the most useful later.
@METHOD | Three methods, one answer: 15% of 240
@LISTO
1. **Fraction method:** $\\frac{15}{100} \\times 240 = \\frac{3600}{100} = 36$.
2. **Ten-percent method:** $10\\%$ of 240 is 24; $5\\%$ is half of that, 12; so $15\\%$ is $24 + 12 = 36$.
3. **Multiplier method:** $15\\% = 0.15$, and $0.15 \\times 240 = 36$.
@KEY | The multiplier is the idea worth keeping
A percentage of an amount is the amount multiplied by the decimal form of the percentage. This is why $15\\%$ of 240 and $240 \\times 0.15$ are the same calculation, and why calculators can handle any percentage in one step.
@TABLE | Percentages of 240 by multiplier
Percentage ; Multiplier ; Calculation ; Result
1% ; 0.01 ; $240 \\times 0.01$ ; 2.4
5% ; 0.05 ; $240 \\times 0.05$ ; 12
10% ; 0.10 ; $240 \\times 0.10$ ; 24
15% ; 0.15 ; $240 \\times 0.15$ ; 36
25% ; 0.25 ; $240 \\times 0.25$ ; 60
@ERR | Dividing by the percentage
“15% of 240” is not $240 \\div 15$. Dividing by 15 finds how many groups of 15 fit into 240, which is a different question. Estimation catches it: 10% of 240 is about 24, so 15% must be a little over 24, not 16.

@EX | 5.2.1 | Percentage of a quantity
GIVEN: 12.5% of 96.
ASKED: Calculate the value using a multiplier.
METHOD: Convert the percentage to a decimal and multiply.
STEPS:
1. $12.5\\% = 0.125$.
2. $0.125 \\times 96 = 12$.
ANSWER: 12.
CHECK: $12.5\\% = \\frac{1}{8}$, and $96 \\div 8 = 12$.

@EX | 5.2.2 | Choosing a method
GIVEN: A restaurant bill of 84 units; a service charge of 17.5% is added.
ASKED: Find the total bill.
METHOD: Find the charge, then add it; or use a single multiplier of 1.175.
STEPS:
1. Charge: $0.175 \\times 84 = 14.7$.
2. Total: $84 + 14.7 = 98.7$.
3. Single step: $84 \\times 1.175 = 98.7$.
ANSWER: 98.7 units.
CHECK: 17.5% is a little under a fifth, and a fifth of 84 is 16.8, so a charge near 15 is reasonable.

@EX | 5.2.3 | Percentage of a percentage problem
GIVEN: In a school of 600 students, 30% are in the first year. Of the first year, 45% take the bus.
ASKED: How many first-year students take the bus?
METHOD: Apply the percentages one at a time, or combine the multipliers.
STEPS:
1. First year: $0.3 \\times 600 = 180$.
2. Bus: $0.45 \\times 180 = 81$.
3. Combined: $0.3 \\times 0.45 = 0.135$, and $0.135 \\times 600 = 81$.
ANSWER: 81 students.
CHECK: 13.5% of 600 is $600 \\times 0.1 + 600 \\times 0.035 = 60 + 21 = 81$.
WATCH: Adding the percentages gives 75%, which is far too large. A percentage of a percentage multiplies.

@SEC | 5.3 | One quantity as a percentage of another | Criterion C · ii, iii
@LEAD | “What percentage of the class are girls?” is a different question from “how many girls are in the class?”. The first is a comparison, and the second is a quantity.
@DEF | Expressing a ratio as a percentage
To express $a$ as a percentage of $b$: divide $a$ by $b$, then multiply by 100.
$\\text{percentage} = \\frac{a}{b} \\times 100\\%$
The amount $b$ is the **base** of the comparison. Changing the base changes the percentage, even when the quantities stay the same.
@KEY | The base always matters
20 out of 25 is $\\frac{20}{25} = 80\\%$, but 20 out of 50 is $\\frac{20}{50} = 40\\%$. The numerator is the same; the base has doubled, so the percentage has halved. Every percentage statement should be read as “this, compared with that”.
@TABLE | The same part, different bases
Part ; Base ; Fraction ; Percentage
18 ; 24 ; $\\frac{3}{4}$ ; 75%
18 ; 36 ; $\\frac{1}{2}$ ; 50%
18 ; 45 ; $\\frac{2}{5}$ ; 40%
18 ; 90 ; $\\frac{1}{5}$ ; 20%
@ERR | Dividing the larger by the smaller
To express 12 as a percentage of 30, the base is 30, so $\\frac{12}{30} = 40\\%$. Writing $\\frac{30}{12} = 250\\%$ answers a different question — what percentage 30 is of 12 — and the wording is what decides. Look for “as a percentage of”.

@EX | 5.3.1 | Expressing a part as a percentage
GIVEN: A football team wins 17 of its 25 matches.
ASKED: Express the wins as a percentage of the matches played.
METHOD: Divide part by base and multiply by 100.
STEPS:
1. $\\frac{17}{25} = 0.68$.
2. $0.68 \\times 100 = 68\\%$.
ANSWER: 68%.
CHECK: $\\frac{17}{25} = \\frac{68}{100}$ by multiplying both parts by 4.

@EX | 5.3.2 | Comparing two percentages
GIVEN: Class A has 18 girls out of 30 students; Class B has 14 girls out of 20.
ASKED: Which class has the higher proportion of girls?
METHOD: Express both as percentages of their own totals.
STEPS:
1. Class A: $\\frac{18}{30} = 60\\%$.
2. Class B: $\\frac{14}{20} = 70\\%$.
3. The counts (18 and 14) cannot be compared directly because the class sizes differ.
ANSWER: Class B, with 70% against 60%.
CHECK: In classes of equal size, the higher count would give the higher percentage.

@EX | 5.3.3 | A percentage change of a percentage
GIVEN: A shop's sales this month are 40% higher than last month; last month they were 20% higher than the month before.
ASKED: Describe the sales this month compared with two months ago.
METHOD: Combine the multipliers in order.
STEPS:
1. Two months ago = 100 units.
2. Last month = $1.2 \\times 100 = 120$.
3. This month = $1.4 \\times 120 = 168$.
ANSWER: Sales are 168% of the level two months ago, an increase of 68%.
CHECK: Adding the percentages gives 60%, but the true figure is 68% because the second increase is calculated on a larger base.
WATCH: Percentages of different bases cannot be added. Convert to amounts or to multipliers first.

@SEC | 5.4 | Percentage increase and decrease | Criterion A · ii; Criterion D · ii
@LEAD | A percentage change is always calculated on the original amount. Using multipliers makes increases and decreases a single multiplication, and combining them becomes simple.
@DEF | Percentage increase and decrease
An increase of $p\\%$ multiplies the original amount by $1 + \\frac{p}{100}$. A decrease of $p\\%$ multiplies by $1 - \\frac{p}{100}$.
An increase of 15% is a multiplier of 1.15; a decrease of 15% is a multiplier of 0.85.
@METHOD | Working with percentage change
@LISTO
1. Identify the original amount — the base.
2. Convert the change to a multiplier.
3. Multiply once. Do not add the change separately unless you need to show it.
4. To find the percentage change between two amounts, divide the change by the **original** amount and multiply by 100.
@TABLE | Multipliers for common changes
Change ; Multiplier ; Example on 200
+5% ; 1.05 ; 210
+20% ; 1.20 ; 240
−10% ; 0.90 ; 180
−25% ; 0.75 ; 150
+100% ; 2.00 ; 400
−100% ; 0 ; 0
@ERR | Calculating the change on the new amount
A 120-unit item increases by 10%. The increase is 12 units, not 12 on 132. When a question reverses direction — “a price rises 10% and then falls 10%” — the fall is calculated on the raised price, so the result is not the original.

@EX | 5.4.1 | A single increase
GIVEN: A rent of 850 units rises by 6%.
ASKED: Find the new rent.
METHOD: Multiply by $1 + 0.06$.
STEPS:
1. Multiplier $= 1.06$.
2. $850 \\times 1.06 = 901$.
ANSWER: 901 units.
CHECK: 6% of 850 is 51, and $850 + 51 = 901$.

@EX | 5.4.2 | A decrease in a shop
GIVEN: A bicycle priced at 280 units is reduced by 15% in a sale, then reduced by a further 20% at the end of the sale.
ASKED: Find the final price and the overall percentage reduction.

3. Combined multiplier: $0.85 \\times 0.8 = 0.68$, so the price is 68% of the original.

@SEC | 5.5 | Discounts, taxes and budgets | Criterion D · i, iii, iv
@LEAD | Real problems rarely announce which quantity is the base. Deciding the base, and keeping track of it through several changes, is the mathematical work.
@KEY | Three questions to ask of any percentage problem
@LISTO
1. **What is the base?** The percentage is always of something; find the amount it compares with.
2. **Is the percentage of the base, or is the base itself unknown?** If the base is unknown, divide by the multiplier rather than multiplying.
3. **How many changes are there, and on which amount is each calculated?** Multiply the multipliers in order.
@METHOD | Finding the original amount
If a price after a 20% increase is 96 units, then $96 = 1.2 \\times \\text{original}$, so the original is $96 \\div 1.2 = 80$. Dividing undoes the multiplication. Estimating the answer before dividing — the original must be less than 96 — prevents most errors.
@TABLE | Working backwards through a change
Situation ; Equation ; Original
After +15% the value is 92 ; $1.15x = 92$ ; 80
After −25% the value is 45 ; $0.75x = 45$ ; 60
After +8% the value is 270 ; $1.08x = 270$ ; 250
After two changes the value is 68 ; $1.7x = 68$ ; 40
@ERR | Dividing by the percentage change
“After a 20% increase the price is 96, so the original was $96 \\times 0.8 = 76.8$” is wrong twice over: it applies the change again and applies it in the same direction. Working backwards divides by the multiplier.

@EX | 5.5.1 | Finding the original price
GIVEN: In a sale, a coat is reduced by 30% and sells for 84 units.
ASKED: Find the original price.
METHOD: Write the relationship and divide by the multiplier.
STEPS:
1. Sale price $= 0.7 \\times$ original.
2. Original $= 84 \\div 0.7 = 120$.
ANSWER: 120 units.
CHECK: 30% of 120 is 36, and $120 - 36 = 84$.

@EX | 5.5.2 | Comparing two offers
GIVEN: Offer 1: 25% off a price of 160 units. Offer 2: 20% off a price of 150 units, then a further 10% off the reduced price.
ASKED: Which offer is cheaper, and by how much?
METHOD: Calculate both final prices with a single multiplier chain.
STEPS:
1. Offer 1: $160 \\times 0.75 = 120$.
2. Offer 2: $150 \\times 0.8 \\times 0.9 = 108$.
3. Difference: $120 - 108 = 12$.
ANSWER: Offer 2 is cheaper by 12 units.
CHECK: As percentages of the marked prices, offer 1 removes 25% and offer 2 removes 28%.

@EX | 5.5.3 | Interpreting a percentage claim
GIVEN: A news report says “the number of visitors rose 200% to 900”.
ASKED: Find the number of visitors before the rise and explain what the percentage means.
METHOD: Interpret 200% as a multiplier of 3.
STEPS:
1. A rise of 200% means the new value is $100\\% + 200\\% = 300\\%$ of the old.
2. $3x = 900$, so $x = 300$.
ANSWER: 300 visitors before; the number tripled.
CHECK: $900 \\div 3 = 300$, and the increase is 600, which is 200% of 300.
WATCH: “Rose 200%” and “rose to 200%” differ by a factor: the first triples, the second doubles.

@DEF | Discount, tax and budget
A **discount** is an amount taken off a marked price, usually quoted as a percentage of it. A **tax** — VAT or sales tax in many countries — is an amount added to a price, also a percentage. A **budget** is a plan for spending a fixed total, and percentages let each item be compared with that total.
@KEY | Adding and removing tax
**Adding tax:** multiply by $1 + \text{rate}$. At 18% VAT, a price of 250 becomes $250 \times 1.18 = 295$.
**Removing tax:** divide by $1 + \text{rate}$, never multiply by $1 - \text{rate}$. If a till receipt of 295 includes 18% VAT, the price before tax is $295 \div 1.18 = 250$, and the tax itself is 45.
Removing tax and taking a discount are different operations: a discount of 18% and a tax of 18% do not cancel, because they are calculated on different amounts.
@TABLE | One price, three ways of quoting it
Case ; Working ; Result
Discount of 20% on 90 ; $90 \times 0.8$ ; 72
Tax of 18% added to 72 ; $72 \times 1.18$ ; 84.96
Tax of 18% included in 84.96 ; $84.96 \div 1.18$ ; 72

@EX | 5.5.4 | Reading a receipt with tax
GIVEN: A receipt shows a total of 236 units, which includes 18% VAT.
ASKED: Find the price before tax and the amount of VAT.
METHOD: Divide by the tax multiplier.
STEPS:
1. $1.18 \times \text{price} = 236$.
2. Price $= 236 \div 1.18 = 200$.
3. VAT $= 236 - 200 = 36$, which is 18% of 200.
ANSWER: 200 units before tax, 36 units of VAT.
CHECK: $200 \times 1.18 = 236$.
WATCH: Taking 18% of 236 gives 42.48, which is too much — the 18% must be taken of the pre-tax price, not of the total.

@EX | 5.5.5 | A budget with percentages
GIVEN: A family budget of 2,400 units a month: 35% housing, 20% food, 15% transport, 10% school costs, and the rest saved.
ASKED: Find each amount and the percentage saved.
METHOD: Convert each percentage to an amount, then check the total.
STEPS:
1. Housing $= 0.35 \times 2400 = 840$; food $= 480$; transport $= 360$; school $= 240$.
2. Spent $= 840 + 480 + 360 + 240 = 1920$.
3. Saved $= 2400 - 1920 = 480$, which is $480 \div 2400 = 20\%$.
ANSWER: 840, 480, 360 and 240 units; 480 units saved, 20% of the budget.
CHECK: $35 + 20 + 15 + 10 + 20 = 100\%$.

@MOVE | G | Reflect and consolidate | Summary, vocabulary, misconceptions and review
@SEC | Review | Consolidating Chapter 5 | Criterion A · i, ii, iii
@H2 | What this chapter established
@LISTU
- A percentage is a fraction over 100; it converts to decimals and fractions by dividing or multiplying by 100.
- A percentage of a quantity is a multiplication by the decimal form.
- A percentage compares a part with a base; changing the base changes the percentage.
- An increase of $p\\%$ multiplies by $1 + \\frac{p}{100}$; a decrease multiplies by $1 - \\frac{p}{100}$.
- Successive percentage changes combine by multiplying multipliers, never by adding percentages.
- Working backwards from a changed amount divides by the multiplier.
@H2 | Essential vocabulary
@TABLE | Vocabulary for Chapter 5
Term ; Meaning in this course
Percentage ; A fraction with denominator 100, written with %.
Base ; The amount a percentage compares with.
Multiplier ; The decimal form of a percentage change, such as 1.15.
Percentage increase ; A rise expressed as a percentage of the original amount.
Percentage decrease ; A fall expressed as a percentage of the original amount.
Complement ; The part that remains, such as 8% when 92% is taken.
Reverse percentage ; Finding the original amount from the changed amount.
@H2 | Common misconceptions
@TABLE | Misconception, correction and evidence
Misconception ; Correction ; Evidence
“Percentages can be added.” ; They apply to different bases. ; $0.8 \\times 0.9 = 0.72$
“A percentage of a quantity means divide by it.” ; Multiply by the decimal form. ; $15\\%$ of 240 is 36
“+20% then −20% returns to the start.” ; The base changes. ; $1.2 \\times 0.8 = 0.96$
“50% more means double.” ; It means half as much again. ; 400 g → 600 g
“After a 20% rise, divide by 20%.” ; Divide by 1.2. ; $96 \\div 1.2 = 80$
